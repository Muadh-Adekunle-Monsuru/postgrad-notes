## Defining Research
* **Core Definition:** Research is a systematic, rigorous, and controlled approach designed to obtain and confirm reliable new knowledge, often explaining what, how, and why events occur.
* **What Research is Not:** Accidental discoveries, mere data collection, or simply searching and reviewing published literature do not constitute research on their own.
* **Methodology vs. Method:** "Research Methodology" encompasses the broad principles for planning, organizing, and validating research, whereas a "Research Method" refers to the specific, field-dependent procedural tasks.
* **Creativity:** Effective research relies heavily on imagination, intuition, curiosity, challenging assumptions, and tolerating uncertainty.


## Philosophy of Science

* **Idealism vs. Materialism:** Idealism argues that reality is dependent on the mind and in constant change, whereas materialism (reductionism) insists that only physical things exist and phenomena are stable.
* **Induction vs. Deduction:** Induction builds general principles from specific, sensory observations (empiricism), while deduction applies general premises to logically predict specific outcomes (rationalism).
* **Hypothetico-Deductive Reasoning:** Proposed by Karl Popper, this "creative middle way" involves identifying a problem, inductively generating a hypothesis, charting its implications deductively, and then testing and refining the theory.
* **Positivism vs. Relativism (Interpretivism):** Positivism treats the world as an objective, discoverable reality requiring a neutral observer and quantitative methods. Conversely, relativism views reality as a mental construct interpreted through personal values, favoring subjective, qualitative methods to understand dynamics.


## The Scientific Method
* **Distinction from Non-Science:** Scientific methods reject intuition, belief, simple consensus, or casual observation.
* **Core Attributes:** The scientific method must be empirically testable, replicable, objective, transparent, falsifiable, and logically consistent.
* **Key Components:** The method operates on the interaction of **Facts** (independent, verifiable observations), **Hypotheses** (testable propositions of relationships), and **Theories** (abstractions and generalizations established by deduction).

## Classification of Research
* **By Application:**
	* *Pure/Basic Research:* Aims to establish fundamental facts and develop theories.
	* *Applied Research:* Utilizes knowledge to solve specific, practical real-life problems.

* **By Objective:**
	* *Descriptive:* Synthesizes information to describe or identify a phenomenon.
	* *Correlational:* Discovers and establishes relationships between two or more variables.
	* *Exploratory:* Investigates areas where very little is currently known.
	* *Explanatory/Analytic:* Establishes exactly why or how a phenomenon occurs.

* **By Mode of Inquiry:**
	* *Qualitative:* Uses nominal or ordinal scales to describe variations and meanings without quantifying magnitude.
	* *Quantitative:* Uses quantitative variables specifically to measure and ascertain the magnitude of variation.

## The Research Process

* **Cyclical Steps:** The research workflow is a circular process that generally flows from identifying a Question/Problem, to Formulating Objectives, Planning and Designing Research, Generating Results, and finally Interpreting Results to derive conclusions.


* **External Influences:** This ongoing cycle is continuously shaped and informed by peers, the public, cross-disciplinary insights, and service expertise.
